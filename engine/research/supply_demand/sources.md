# Supply/demand and support/resistance validity: what has actually been measured

Compiled 2026-10-30 from the pages themselves (PDFs extracted with pdftotext). Credibility scale: **A** peer-reviewed / central-bank; **B** arXiv preprint; **C** vendor or hobbyist backtest with stated method and numbers; **D** assertion / anecdote, no measurement.

## 1. Osler (2000) "Support for Resistance: Technical Analysis and Intraday Exchange Rates", FRBNY Economic Policy Review 6(2)
URL: https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf
Method: S/R levels published daily by six FX firms, Jan 1996–Mar 1998, DEM/JPY/GBP vs USD; Reuters quotes at 1-minute intervals 9am–4pm NY. "Hit" = bid/ask within 0.01% of the level; "bounce" = rate is back on the near side of the level 15 minutes later. Bootstrap against 10,000 sets of arbitrary levels per day (20 S + 20 R, uniform within the month's typical daily range).
Numbers: arbitrary levels bounced **56.2%**; published levels **60.8%** (Table 8). Published beat arbitrary in all 16 firm-currency pairs; by currency +4.2pp mark, +5.6pp yen, +4.0pp pound. Best firm (Firm 1) yen: **66.5%** vs 57.3% arbitrary (+9.2pp). Robust to 0.00%/0.02% hit bands and a 30-minute window. Five days after publication the levels still beat arbitrary ones but bounce rate was **1.7pp lower** than on publication day (Table 10). Firms' own "strength" ratings (1/2/3) had **no consistent predictive value** (Table 12: Firm 1 strength-2 minus strength-1 = -2.3, -4.1, -6.5pp). Agreement between firms: ~30% of possible matches; levels agreed by several firms were **not** more reliable. 96% of published levels end in 0 or 5; 00 and 50 "disproportionately represented". No firm reported anything like base/departure rules.
Credibility: **A**. Note the base rate: a random level "holds" ~56% under this 15-min definition, so a raw 60% hit rate is only ~5pp of edge.

## 2. Osler (2003) "Currency Orders and Exchange Rate Dynamics", J. Finance 58(5) 1791–1819 (working paper: FRBNY Staff Report 125, 2001)
URL: https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf
Method: 9,667 stop-loss and take-profit orders at one large dealing bank, 1 Sep 1999–11 Apr 2000 (43% USDJPY, 33% EURUSD, 24% GBPUSD). Distribution of requested execution rates by last two digits; bootstrap tests; then simulated exchange rates driven by clustered orders tested against 10,000 arbitrary levels.
Numbers: single largest cluster at rates ending **00: ~8.7% of all orders** (uniform would be 1%); then 50, other 0s, then 5s; no cluster at 25/75. **Take-profit orders at exactly 00: 9.3%; stop-loss: 4.4%.** On a rise into a 00 level, ~2.8% of all stop-loss buys trigger vs ~10.5% of take-profit sells, so flow at the level is reflecting (Prediction 1: reversal). **Stop-loss buys: 7.4% at digits 90–99 vs 14.4% at 01–10** (just above the round number); stop-loss sells mirror this just below; take-profits show no asymmetry (3 of 4 stop-loss asymmetries significant at 5%, the fourth at 6.4%). Hence Prediction 2: trends accelerate after a round number is crossed. Companion paper "Stop-loss orders and price cascades" (JF 2005) documents the cascade.
Credibility: **A**. The only microstructure evidence for *why* a level holds or breaks; it supports round-number levels, not pattern-defined zones.

## 3. Classic academic S/R and pattern studies
**Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992)**, J. Finance 47(5) 1731–64. https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html. DJIA 1897–1986, moving-average and trading-range-break rules; returns after buy signals exceed those after sell signals, sell-signal returns negative, bootstrap-robust; 26 rules in all. No transaction costs. I could not open the full table, so no per-rule number is quoted here. Credibility **A** (later work finds it fades post-1986, and a NASDAQ replication found no TRB power).
**Curcio & Goodhart (1992)**, LSE FMG DP142, https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/119184. Three FX series; trading breaks of chartist-identified S/R was profitable after costs, more so than mechanical rules. Credibility **A-** (working paper, 1992 data).
**Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000)**, J. Finance 55, 1705–65, https://www.nber.org/papers/w7613. Kernel-regression pattern detection, US stocks 1962–96; conditional return distributions differ from unconditional for several patterns ("incremental information", not profits). Not about S/R levels. Credibility **A**.
**Zapranis & Tsinaslanidis (2012)**, Applied Financial Economics 22(19) 1571–85, DOI 10.1080/09603107.2012.663469. Rule-based horizontal S/R from local extremes, NASDAQ/NYSE daily, ~20 years. Support predicts trend interruptions better than resistance; **neither beats buy-and-hold**. Credibility **A** (abstract only read; full text paywalled).
**Garzarelli et al. (2014)** "Memory effects in stock price dynamics", Scientific Reports 4:4487, https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.5197. 9 LSE stocks, tick data, all of 2002; a level = prior local extreme with a stripe; bounce vs cross counted at 45/60/90/180-second sampling. Shuffled-return series bounce ≈ **0.50**; real series **≈0.55–0.62**, and p(bounce | n previous bounces) **rises with n** (chi-square p<0.0001 at 45/60/90 s, not at 180 s). Credibility **A**.
**Chung & Bellotti (2021)** "Evidence and Behaviour of Support and Resistance Levels in Financial Time Series", arXiv:2101.07410, https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07410. 1-minute data for all of 2018: **EURUSD**, Lloyds, Brent. Levels from rolling-window extremes (60- and 240-min lag windows); 1,000 shuffled-return series as null. Bounce probability is above the shuffled 0.5 and **increases with previous bounces** (permutation-test Λ≈1.000 for bprev 1–6 on EURUSD, Table 3.1). Decay: for EURUSD a level with 1 previous bounce decays to p=0.5 at ~**350-minute** lag window, with 4 previous bounces at ~**900 minutes**; Brent 1-bounce levels decay below 0.5 within ~300 min. Credibility **B** (preprint, one year, no costs, but directly FX intraday).
Takeaway: academic S/R work consistently finds **more touches = stronger**, the opposite of the "fresh zone" doctrine, and that the edge is a few percentage points over a ~50–56% base rate.

## 4. Backtests of order blocks / FVG / SMC (no peer-reviewed paper exists as of this search)
Searches of arXiv/SSRN for "order block", "fair value gap", "smart money concepts" returned nothing academic. What exists:
**TradingView "Order Block (OB) Statistics: Real Win Rate vs Random Levels"** (open source), https://www.tradingview.com/script/yFmy3VBi-Order-Block-OB-Statistics-Real-Win-Rate-vs-Random-Levels/. OB = last opposite candle before a close-based BOS, usable only after swing confirmation; hit = symmetric ±kR target/stop from first touch; **placebo controls** matched by height, direction, distance. 31 runs, BTCUSDT/EURUSD/ES, 4 timeframes. Control baseline **47.3–52.3%** (not 50%). EURUSD 30m: **-4.9pp** vs control (z=-2.69) but **+0.1pp** BTC, **+0.8pp** ES; pooled **-2.10pp** (CI -4.3 to +0.1); mean of 23 estimates -0.87pp; 2-ATR displacement filter: 46.7% hit, -0.6pp vs control. Conclusion: "did not detect a reliable positive or negative order-block effect". Credibility **C+** (best method found; code public).
**StatOasis "I Backtested ICT / SMC: What Survives"**, https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives. SPY/QQQ/DIA/IWM daily, 648 variants, 50-seed random-entry baseline. OB (down-close bar + impulse ≥ M×ATR20, retrace entry): SPY 5-day forward edge **+0.12%, t=1.22**; FVG (3-bar gap, 1,122 events) **-0.005%, t=-0.08**; only 1 of 32 t-stats ≥2. **0 of 648 beat buy-and-hold.** Daily bars only. Credibility **C**.
**indicatoredge.io SMC page**, https://indicatoredge.io/smart-money. 738 assets, cost-included, out-of-sample: Order Block median win **40.0%**, alpha vs B&H **-6.5%**, beats B&H on 12% of assets; FVG 42.8%, -7.7%; Liquidity sweep 71.9% win yet -7.4% alpha. No code, no period stated. Credibility **C-**.
**Backtrex GBPUSD order-block test**, https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/order-block-gbp-usd. H1, Oct 2016–Oct 2026, first-touch entry, EMA-50 filter: **203 trades, 38.4% win, PF 0.83**, DD -27.6%. Product marketing, but negative. Credibility **C-**.
**FVG fill rate**: only self-reported indicator output: QQQ daily, 1,694 gaps, **93% filled**, mean 54.8 bars (TradingView "Fair Value Gap Screener [ITA]"). Medium "I backtested 2,600 SMC trades" (~70% FVG fill) was not readable (403). Credibility **D**.
**Fresh vs tested / base count / departure / time at level**: no backtest found that isolates these. The only numbers are a prop-firm blog's **personal journal**: fresh 62%, first retest 48%, second retest 33% over "200+ futures trades" (https://proptradingvibes.com/blog/supply-and-demand-trading, credibility **D**). The OB-statistics script's 2-ATR displacement filter did not help. Academic S/R says tested levels are *stronger*.

## 5. Vendor rule sets
**Sam Seiden / OTA odds enhancers** (reconstructed from forum/blog; OTA's points table is not public): strength of departure, time at the base (fewer candles better), freshness/first return, profit margin (≥3:1 to first target), higher-timeframe trend, position on the curve; rated out of 10, trade ≥8. Sources: https://forums.babypips.com/t/odd-enhancers-sam-seiden/39321, https://www.eltraderfinanciado.com/en/blog/supply-and-demand-zones. No measured results anywhere; FTC settled with OTA in 2020 over earnings claims. Credibility **D**.
**Set and Forget / Alfonso Moreno**: same rally-base-rally etc. grammar, limit order at proximal edge, stop beyond distal, imbalance (FVG inside the departure) as a quality mark. No published statistics found. Credibility **D**.
**Trading Rush**: no "supply and demand tested 100 times" post exists on tradingrush.net. Closest: "Never sell at this resistance (tested 200 times)", https://tradingrush.net/why-you-should-never-sell-at-this-resistance-tested-200-times/ — daily S&P resistance (reversal >10%), shorting at resistance in an uptrend **50%** win vs **~44%** for random shorts; the author's own earlier 200-trade random-entry control. Credibility **C-** (manual, one market).
**ForexFactory / BabyPips**: threads assert fresh > tested and 70–90% for "strong fresh zones" (NexusFi) with no tally. Credibility **D**.

## 6. LuxAlgo / TradingView definitions
**Order Blocks & Breaker Blocks [LuxAlgo]**, https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/order-blocks-breaker-blocks/: OB seeded from a swing point (Swing Lookback), zone from full range or candle body ("Use Candle Body"); mitigated when "price trades through the zone", at which point it is redrawn as a breaker with opposite polarity. The public page does not state close vs wick; the sibling "Order Blocks" and "eBacktesting Order Blocks" scripts expose a **Mitigation = Close | Wick** setting ("Close" needs a candle close beyond the zone, "Wick" clears on any touch), and the Signals & Alerts version offers Proximal / 50% / Distal mitigation levels.
**Supply and Demand Visible Range [LuxAlgo]**, https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/supply-and-demand-visible-range/: not pattern-based. Visible range split into N bins; volume (intrabar data) accumulated from range high downward = supply, from range low upward = demand, stopping at Threshold % of total volume; solid line = area average, dashed = volume-weighted average. No mitigation logic; recalculates on scroll. Credibility: documentation only, no performance claim.

## Criterion table

| Criterion | Measured support | Asserted only |
|---|---|---|
| Freshness (untested zone better) | None. Garzarelli 2014, Chung & Bellotti 2021 measure the **opposite** (more prior bounces, higher bounce probability); Osler 2000 levels persisted 5 days | Seiden/OTA, Set & Forget, prop-firm journal (62/48/33%), forums |
| Base candle count / time at level | None | OTA ("fewer candles"), prop blog (2–5 candles) |
| Departure strength / displacement | OB-statistics script: 2-ATR filter gave no edge vs placebo; StatOasis M×ATR variants t<2 | OTA, ICT, every vendor |
| BOS / structure break as entry trigger | indicatoredge: BOS median win 46.9%, -5.4% alpha; OB-stat script uses close-based BOS, no edge | ICT/SMC |
| FVG | StatOasis: 1,122 SPY FVG retraces, forward edge ≈0 (t=-0.08); indicatoredge 42.8% win, -7.7% alpha; fill rate 93% on QQQ daily (unverified) | "70% fill", kill-zone FVGs |
| Time at level (short dwell = strong) | None | OTA |
| Round numbers | Osler 2000 (96% of pro levels end 0/5), Osler 2003 (8.7% of orders at 00; TP cluster at 00, SL just beyond) | — |
| Session / time of day | Osler 2000: results unchanged morning vs afternoon; nothing else measured | Backtrex "kill-zone FVGs revisit more" |
| Horizontal S/R level in general | Osler 2000 (+4.6pp over 56.2% base), Garzarelli, Chung & Bellotti, Curcio & Goodhart | — |

Honest summary: the only measured edges are (a) professionally chosen horizontal levels and round numbers in FX, worth ~4–9pp over a ~56% 15-minute bounce base rate, decaying over days intraday and over hours for rolling-window levels; (b) prior bounces raise bounce probability. Every SMC-specific rule (fresh zone, base count, departure strength, FVG, OB mitigation by close vs wick) is folklore with no measured result, and the three independent OB/FVG backtests found no edge versus placebo levels or buy-and-hold.
